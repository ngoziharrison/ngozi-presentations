A discipline consists of people, institutions, journals, norms, methods, knowledge practices, and a set of canonical problems for inquiry

# What is Critical Data Studies
	> “In sum, we are starting to witness the development of what Dalton and Thatcher (2014) call critical data studies -- research and thinking that applies critical social theory to data to explore the ways in which they are never simply neutral, objective, independent, raw representations of the world, but are situated, contingent, relational, contextual, and do active work in the world.” (Kitchin and Lauriault, 2014, p. 5)
---
# Another Definition
	> “A critical approach to Big Data investigates meta-theoretical modes of conversation and styles of scientific thinking (Hacking, 1994) that pervade data science—it does not contribute to knowledge in the positivistic sense but instead analyzes the ground upon which positivistic Big Data science stands.” (Iliadis and Russo, 2016, p. 2)

# This is a class about technology, but it is also a reading and writing class
	Not a *data science* class but a *critical data studies* class
We are interested in the history, social aspects, and ethics of data and how what we do with data affects how we think about each other and society

---

# Guiding questions for the course
	- What is data? Where does data come from? How is data produced? 
	- When and why are data sometimes accepted at face value or treated as fact? 
	- What are the values embedded in a data-based claim or a data-driven computational model?


---
# More Guiding Questions
	- How do data collection and data analysis intersect with histories of exclusion?
	- What is the relationship between data, truth, and disinformation? 
	- How does the proliferation of data technologies, machine learning algorithms, artificial intelligence (AI), and information collection systems impact contemporary life?
	- How can data be mobilized for justice, equity, and the common good?
